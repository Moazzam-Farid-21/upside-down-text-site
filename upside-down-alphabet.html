Shaded boxes are characters that look the same, or have no separate Unicode twin, when turned upside down.

Why some letters look the same

A few letters are symmetrical when rotated half a turn, so their upside down form is just the same letter: o, s, x, z, H, I, N, O, S, X, Z, and the numbers 0 and 8. Other capitals such as B, D and K have no true rotated twin in Unicode, so this chart leaves them as they are rather than inventing a poor match.

Which characters are approximations

Most lowercase flips come from the International Phonetic Alphabet, where the shapes match closely. Several capitals borrow symbols from math and other blocks, for example ∀ for A and ⊥ for T, which look right but are not perfect in every font. How a character looks also depends on the device, and some older apps show an empty box instead. If that happens, our guide on why upside down text shows boxes explains the fix.
